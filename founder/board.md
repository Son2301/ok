# Board summary: Minh AI

**Vote: 3 fund if, 0 pass. Average score 4.3 / 10** (Offers 5, Monopoly 4, Product 4).

Memos: [offers](board/offers.md) · [monopoly](board/monopoly.md) · [product](board/product.md) · [brief](board/brief.md)

## Risks every member raised

1. **Four products, no focus.** All three call this the top or second-biggest threat. Four buyers (developers, shop owners, families, API businesses) need four offers, four first-five-minutes experiences and four channels, and one founder is building all of them. "Four AI products on one wallet" describes the architecture, not a product.
2. **The supply side can switch it off.** Anthropic doesn't permit unapproved resale, VAT on resold tokens is unresolved, and Zalo can reprice. The Gateway margin is supposed to fund everything, free Safe included, so if that margin goes, the rest falls with it.
3. **Nothing has been verified live or tested on customers.** No live SePay, provider, Zalo or GitHub integration. No user research, prices, willingness-to-pay data, CAC, budget or runway. For a brand whose promise is "verifiably honest", unverified plumbing undermines the core claim.

## Where they disagree

- **Can the honesty be copied?** The Offers lens sees receipts, never-expiring credit and caps as cheap to deliver and highly valuable, which makes them a strong offer. The Monopoly lens says a funded local rival could copy them within six months. In its view, the only part that lasts is the authenticity-probe record, built up into a dataset over time.
- **Which wedge?** Offers and Product both point to wallet + Gateway for developers. Monopoly accepts that or **Review on its own**, partly because Review doesn't depend on reselling one provider's models in the same way.
- **What counts as proof?** Offers wants prepayment from named prospects and a written guarantee. Product wants to watch 10–20 real users through their first five minutes. Monopoly wants a measured CAC and a market share target in a market whose size nobody knows yet.

## Conditions checklist

- [ ] Pick **one** lead product and state it in one sentence. Freeze the other three until it has paying users. *(all three)*
- [ ] Get resale rights in writing for every provider sold, or redesign Gateway without them. *(all three)* → legal
- [ ] Settle VAT on resold tokens and the PDPL basis for scam blacklists before scaling. *(Offers, Monopoly)* → `/founder-cfo`, legal
- [ ] Verify live, end to end: one VietQR/SePay top-up, one routed call through a provider that permits resale, and a receipt in đồng. *(all three)*
- [ ] Set real prices and gross margin per product after provider cost, VAT and fees. *(all three)* → `/founder-pricing`, `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] Run a willingness-to-pay test with 10–20 named prospects; some must prepay. *(Offers)* → `/founder-consumer`, then real buyers
- [ ] Watch 10–20 target users go through the first five minutes and record where they hesitate. *(Product)*
- [ ] Measure CAC and margin on a first paying cohort. *(Monopoly)* → `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] Write a named guarantee with a cost cap the margin can cover. *(Offers)* → `/founder-offer`
- [ ] Publish authenticity probes on a schedule and build them into a lasting dataset. *(Monopoly, Offers)*
- [ ] Write down the budget and runway, and how long Gateway can fund free Safe. *(all three)* → `/founder-cfo`

## The strongest version the board can see

It is not the four-product company you pitched. It is **one product: verified-authentic AI access for Vietnamese developers, paid in đồng.** That means a VietQR wallet, a Vietnamese e-invoice, a receipt for every call, and a public, dated authenticity record showing which model actually answered, sold only on supply you are licensed to resell. It wins against grey-market resellers on trust rather than price, and against global gateways on local payment and invoicing. Review is the natural second product, because it sells to the same developer and its PR footers bring in new ones. Agents and Safe are good ideas for later. Building them now would spread one founder too thin on exactly the details the strategy depends on. If resale rights can't be secured, the board's fallback wedge is Review on its own.
